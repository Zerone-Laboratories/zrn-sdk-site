How the shell draws

ztMETRO is the shell and the Wayland compositor in one. Every frame it draws the whole screen in one GLES2 pass, layer by layer, bottom to top, in a fixed order. Your app is one of those layers.

02 frost stripsfrost[0..1]

03 app windowwl_surface texture

04 keyboardosk_layer

05 status barstatus_layer

06 action centreshade_layer

07 nav barnav_layer

08 lock screenlock_layer

09 callztphone over the lock

10 volume and toastsvolume_layer, toast_layer

The picture is ours: each layer is drawn with zrnui's controls by gen/layers.c (GLES2, offscreen, demo content) at the place and size ztMETRO gives it, then laid out in perspective by gen/layers.py. It shows what each layer is, not the shell's own pixels. The order and the names come from render_frame in ztmetro/ztmetro.c (ztOSMobile, read on 21 November 2026).

Apart and back

The frame you see fans out into its ten layers and folds back. The layers that are not in this frame (frost, the action centre, the lock screen, the call, the volume) fade in as they move apart.

The boot splash is left out too. One exception to "one pass": when your app is at rest and fills its area, the shell may put its buffer straight on a display plane (plane_pick) instead of drawing it, if the display hardware accepts it.

Textures and what is drawn each frame

  • App windows are textures already: the shell gets your buffer from Wayland and draws it, it does not copy it.
  • The shell's own layers (the bars, the keyboard, the action centre, the lock screen, the banners, each tile) are painted once into a texture and uploaded again only when what they show changes.
  • Each frame the shell only places those textures: a quad each, with the position, turn and fade of the moment. The frost strips are blurred on the GPU only when what is under the bars has changed (every frame only during the turn); the call's frosted backdrop is the other blur.

The shell moves the apps

Because every app is a layer of the same frame, the shell, not the app, animates going between them: Start's tiles turning away and an app swinging in (the turnstile), an app swinging out on Back, the switcher's cards, the launch flip in landscape, and the turn of the screen. Every app arrives and leaves the same way, and an app needs no code for it.

What it means for your app

  • Your window is one textured layer. Draw your whole page into it, at 60 frames a second when it moves. The shell does not draw anything inside it.
  • Your app bar is yours; the keyboard is the shell's. zrnui draws the app bar inside your window, at the bottom of your page. The on-screen keyboard is a shell layer over your window: zrnui gives your page the room it takes (the keyboard inset, see Coming next).
  • Frost samples what is under it. The shell's frost blurs only Start and the list under its bars; over your app the bars are opaque. A frosted element in your app (Zelement_set_frosted_diffusion) blurs what your own page drew under it, never other apps or the shell.
  • Calls, the nav bar and the volume are always on top. Nothing your app draws can cover them, and you do not need to leave room for the volume banner or a toast: they come and go over you.
  • You do not animate your arrival. Launch, Back and the turn are drawn by the shell. Page transitions inside your app (the turnstile between pages, the pivot) are zrnui's, see Animations.
